How to clean and care for wooden furniture

Simple, safe ways to clean and maintain solid wood, veneer and laminate furniture so it looks good for years — what to use and what to avoid.

Care & maintenance2 min read17 Nov 2026By Mumbai Interior team

This article is in English.

In this article
  1. Daily and weekly cleaning
  2. Solid wood and veneer
  3. Laminate
  4. Acrylic and high-gloss
  5. Things to avoid
  6. Hardware care
  7. Termites and borers

Good furniture can last decades if it is looked after. Most care is simple and takes only a few minutes a week. The right method depends on the finish, so start by knowing what your furniture is made of.

Daily and weekly cleaning

  • Dust regularly with a soft, dry microfibre cloth. Dust is abrasive and dulls the finish over time.
  • Wipe spills immediately with a damp, well-wrung cloth, then dry the area.
  • Avoid dripping-wet mopping around furniture legs.

Solid wood and veneer

  1. Use a cloth slightly damp with water and a drop of mild soap for marks. Dry immediately.
  2. Avoid harsh cleaners, ammonia and alcohol, which can damage polish.
  3. Re-polish when the surface looks dull or patchy — usually every few years, depending on use.
  4. Keep furniture out of strong direct sunlight, which fades and dries wood.

Laminate

Laminate is the easiest to care for. Wipe with a damp cloth and mild detergent. For stubborn marks, a soft cloth with a little diluted cleaner is fine. Avoid scrubbing pads, which leave scratches on glossy laminate.

Acrylic and high-gloss

Use only a soft microfibre cloth and water or a cleaner made for acrylic. Paper towels and rough cloths cause fine scratches that show on gloss.

Test any new cleaner on a hidden spot first, such as the inside of a door.

Things to avoid

AvoidWhy
Hot vessels directly on woodLeaves white heat marks
Wet glasses without coastersWater rings
Strong chemicals and thinnerStrips polish
Dragging furnitureBreaks joints and scratches floors
Placing against damp wallsFungus and swelling

Hardware care

Tighten handle screws when they loosen, keep sliding wardrobe tracks free of dust, and adjust hinges if doors stop aligning. A little attention prevents bigger repairs.

Termites and borers

Look out for small holes, fine powder below furniture, or mud lines on walls. Act quickly — read our termite protection guide.
