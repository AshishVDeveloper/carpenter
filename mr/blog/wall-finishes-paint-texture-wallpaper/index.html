Wall finishes compared: paint, texture, wallpaper and panelling

Emulsion paint, texture, wallpaper and wall panelling compared for Indian homes — looks, cost, durability in humidity and where each works best.

Materials & finishes2 min read17 Nov 2026By Mumbai Interior team

This article is in English.

  2. Texture paint
  3. Wallpaper
  4. Wall panelling
  5. Comparison

Walls are the largest surface in any room, so the finish sets the mood. Here is a simple comparison of the four main options for homes in Mumbai.

Emulsion paint

The most common finish. Modern emulsions come in matt, satin and sheen finishes.

  • Affordable and easy to repaint.
  • Washable emulsions are worth it in kitchens, kids' rooms and corridors.
  • In humid areas, choose an anti-fungal paint and fix dampness first.

Texture paint

Texture creates patterns — sand, stucco, rustic or designer effects — on one feature wall.

  • Hides minor wall imperfections.
  • Best used on one wall behind a bed or sofa; too much texture feels busy.
  • Harder to repaint over later.

Wallpaper

Modern wallpapers include vinyl and non-woven types with thousands of designs.

  • Quick to install and makes a big visual change.
  • Choose vinyl wallpaper in humid rooms; avoid paper wallpaper near bathrooms and kitchens.
  • Must go on a dry, smooth wall — dampness causes peeling.

Wall panelling

Panels in laminate, veneer, fluted MDF or PVC give a premium, hotel-like look.

  • Hides wiring and uneven walls.
  • Great behind beds and TV units — see our TV unit ideas.
  • Costs more than paint but lasts long and needs little upkeep.

Comparison

फिनिशRelative costHumidityBest for
EmulsionLowGood with anti-fungal paintAll walls
TextureMediumGoodOne feature wall
WallpaperMediumVinyl onlyBedrooms, living rooms
PanellingHighGood with right materialTV wall, bed back

Fix seepage and dampness before any finish. Every option — even expensive panelling — will fail on a wet wall.

Choosing colours

Light, warm neutrals make small rooms feel larger. Use deeper colours on one wall, and repeat a colour from your furniture or curtains so the room feels connected.
